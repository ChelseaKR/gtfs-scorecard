# Research and ideation sweep: route performance and progressive maps

Date: 2026-07-10

## Evidence reviewed

- Mobile Lighthouse runs against `/agencies/`, `/map/`, and `/pulse/`.
- The generated page shell and the national-map rendering path.
- Current [GTFS Schedule Best Practices](https://gtfs.org/documentation/schedule/schedule-best-practices/),
  [accessibility guidance](https://gtfs.org/getting-started/features/accessibility/),
  [Pathways examples](https://gtfs.org/documentation/schedule/examples/pathways/),
  and the [April 2026 specification revision history](https://gtfs.org/documentation/schedule/change-history/revision-history/).
- Current [FTA National Transit Database guidance](https://www.transit.dot.gov/ntd)
  and [MobilityData validator releases](https://github.com/MobilityData/gtfs-validator/releases).
- The product roadmap, follow-up list, and earlier visualization backlog.

The clearest user-facing regression was the national map. It eagerly downloaded
the MapLibre runtime, style, and tiles even though an equivalent accessible table
was already the primary interface. Its mobile Lighthouse result was 33, with
1.7 seconds of total blocking time. Generated pages also contacted Google Fonts
before rendering, despite having complete system-font fallbacks.

## Ideas considered

1. Remove third-party web-font requests and keep the existing system stacks.
2. Load the national map only after a person asks for it.
3. Paginate or virtualize the largest agency and program tables.
4. Add a validator/spec compatibility guard for newly valid optional fields.
5. Publish a machine-readable GTFS specification-change radar.
6. Add a Pathways and station-accessibility depth view.
7. Expand performance regression checks to representative data-heavy routes.
8. Correct stale operational documentation left by the S3 cutover.

## Decision

This batch implements ideas 1, 2, 7, and 8. They address measured friction,
preserve the static architecture, and do not create another product surface.
The map remains a useful visual enhancement, while the agency table loads and
filters without downloading mapping code or tiles. A visible control explains
the data cost and reports loading or failure without moving focus.

The post-change local mobile run improved the map performance score from 33 to
87. Total blocking time fell from 1.7 seconds to zero and largest contentful
paint fell from 8.1 to 3.3 seconds. The agency directory improved from 66 to 82
after removing the font request. Lighthouse scores vary by machine, so the
automated contract tests the resource-loading behavior directly while the route
measurements remain review evidence.

Ideas 3 and 4 remain good follow-ups but need separate correctness work: table
windowing must preserve find-in-page and assistive-technology behavior, and a
spec guard needs an explicit validator-version policy. Idea 5 is useful only if
it can drive an operational alert rather than another page to maintain. Idea 6
would duplicate accessibility depth that already appears in the national
adoption view and agency scorecards.

## Approval checks

- The agency table is usable before, during, and after map loading.
- The map canvas stays hidden from assistive technology and outside the tab order.
- Loading and failure are announced through a polite status region.
- The control has a full-width mobile treatment and a retry state.
- No generated page requires a third-party font request for its first render.
- Representative heavy routes are measured before and after. Accessibility
  remains at 100, map blocking time is removed, and neither route regresses.
