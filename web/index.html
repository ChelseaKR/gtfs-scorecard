The line

From a feed URL to a fix, in four stops.

No setup. We track public GTFS feeds in the countries currently covered. Find yours and read the grade.

STOP 01

We fetch your published feed

The same GTFS (opens in a new tab) file trip planners read. Scheduled once a day, politely, straight from the URL you already publish; the status page records actual completion.

STOP 02

We run the canonical validator

The MobilityData GTFS validator (opens in a new tab) used across the GTFS ecosystem. We score on top of it; we don't reinvent the rules.

STOP 03

We translate it to plain language

A letter grade from the categories the feed can support: correctness, freshness, rider experience, and, when measurable, realtime. Every finding is rewritten as a plain-language fix with an effort hint, never a failure.

STOP 04

You get the three things to do first

Ordered by what hurts riders most. An expired feed comes before a cosmetic gap. Start at the top; one fix is a real win.

One standard, local context

The quality core travels. Policy layers stay local.

Every covered feed gets the same GTFS checks and respectful plain-language fixes. Country-specific reporting rules and demographic overlays appear only where their source data applies.

Worldwide GTFS core

One disclosed rubric

Correctness, freshness, rider experience, and optional realtime quality mean the same thing wherever a feed is published.

Country-first discovery

Browse the coverage we actually have

Countries and ISO subdivisions are first-class filters. Missing places are shown as not covered, never treated as poor performers.

Regional modules

Local rules are clearly scoped

For the United States, separate NTD readiness and equity tools remain available without defining the global product.

The scorecard measures published data, not legal compliance or service quality. New regional guidance can be added without changing the worldwide GTFS core.

Who it's for

Three roles, one screen.

The person who runs an agency and the people who support or study agencies at scale face the same question. One page answers it for all of them.

The transit manager
